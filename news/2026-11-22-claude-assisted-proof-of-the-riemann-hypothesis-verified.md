**Claude-Assisted Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis Verified**

Why: The Riemann hypothesis, posed in 1859 and one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize problems, has been among the most famous open questions in mathematics, and within about a day of Levent Alpöge publishing his proof, the Lean and Mathlib communities certified it as correct. The verification is itself part of the story: the 17-million-line Lean library was compiled and checked by several independent proof checkers, and MIT's Andrew Sutherland published a detailed log of the checks. Alpöge says Claude did much of the work near the finish line, which makes this the clearest case yet of AI helping crack a landmark problem, and it arrives a day after Anthropic's record listing and in the middle of an AI market rout. Reading in depth explains what was proved, how a machine-checked proof can be trusted, and why some mathematicians remain uneasy about wealthy AI companies racing to claim famous problems.
